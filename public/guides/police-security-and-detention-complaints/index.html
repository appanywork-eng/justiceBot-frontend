Security and law enforcement · Nigerian complaint guidance

Police, security-agency and unlawful-detention complaints

Record a security complaint carefully, prioritise urgent safety, identify the correct command or oversight authority and protect sensitive personal information.

By PetitionDesk Editorial Team · Reviewed 25 August 2026

Who this guide is for: People reporting police conduct, detention concerns, security-agency misconduct, extortion allegations or a failure to respond to a complaint.

Understand the problem before writing

A security complaint requires careful separation of an immediate emergency from a later accountability request. If someone faces present danger, a missing-person emergency or an urgent medical risk, seek immediate assistance through the appropriate emergency and legal-support channels rather than waiting for a drafted petition.

For a complaint about police or security personnel, identify the agency, command, station, location and dates where those details are known. Record the conduct actually observed and distinguish the complainant's account from allegations that require independent investigation. Avoid naming an officer unless there is a reliable basis for the identification.

Detention complaints should identify who is affected, the last confirmed location, the agency believed to be involved, the time of arrest or detention and whether legal or family access was requested. Do not claim that a detention is unlawful solely because the complete legal context is not yet known; explain the concern and request verification, access and a lawful review.

Oversight routes may include the relevant police command, a police disciplinary authority, the National Human Rights Commission or the Public Complaints Commission, depending on the complaint. Copying unrelated agencies can increase privacy exposure without improving accountability.

Build an evidence checklist

Use records that relate directly to your complaint. Put events in date order, identify the organisation involved, and keep copies of every message or acknowledgement. Describe what your documents actually show; do not present an allegation as a proven finding.

  • Agency, station, command or incident location
  • Dates and factual description of the alleged conduct
  • Name or description of the affected person where appropriate
  • Any arrest, complaint, case or station reference
  • Available witness details or lawful supporting evidence
  • Immediate safety, medical or access concerns

Choose the institution that can respond

Where safe and appropriate, report the matter to the responsible security command or official complaint channel. For human-rights concerns, consider the competent rights or oversight institution and request documented investigation, lawful access or a clear status update.

A company mentioned in a statement, address, receipt or background document is not automatically the institution responsible for the complaint. Identify who provided the disputed service, who made the decision, and who can supply records or correct the problem.

Decide whether escalation is justified

If the responsible authority is implicated, unresponsive or unable to provide protection, use the oversight route that matches the factual allegations and urgency. Domestic and international escalation are separate decisions; international procedures usually require a documented basis and admissibility review.

Before escalating, keep the original complaint reference, the institution's written answer where available, and a concise explanation of what remains unresolved. Check the current instructions published by the relevant authority instead of assuming that one deadline applies to every type of complaint.

Worked example: applying the route in practice

A family seeking information about a detained relative should state the last confirmed contact, agency or station believed to be involved, date of detention, requests already made and any urgent legal or medical concern.

Avoid common mistakes and protect personal data

Do not publish a vulnerable person's full identity documents, make unsupported accusations against unnamed officers or use a public petition as a substitute for urgent legal representation or emergency assistance.

Remove passwords, one-time passcodes, card security codes and unrelated medical, financial or family information. Request a specific remedy supported by your evidence, such as an explanation, correction, investigation, refund or written decision.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if someone is in immediate danger?

Use the appropriate emergency, medical or legal-assistance channel immediately. A written complaint is not an emergency-response service.

Should every security complaint be copied to multiple agencies?

No. Include only the command and oversight institutions with a genuine connection to the incident, particularly where disclosure could expose a vulnerable person.
